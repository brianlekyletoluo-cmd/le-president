The Parks & Gardens of Nairobi

One story at a time: origins, annual events, constraints, rules, and the people behind Nairobi’s green spaces.

Explore the Parks

Nairobi Parks

Each park is presented with its origin, annual events, constraints, and rules.

Park 01

Nairobi National Park

Origin: Established in 1946, Nairobi National Park was pioneered by conservationist Mervyn Cowie, who became its first director. It remains a unique wildlife park located close to Nairobi’s city centre.

Annual events: World Wildlife Day activities, World Tourism Day celebrations, conservation education, wildlife awareness programmes, and guided conservation visits.

Constraints: Entry fees, designated roads, limited access to some conservation zones, weather conditions, and protection of wildlife habitats.

Rules: Visitors must follow Kenya Wildlife Service regulations, remain in designated areas, avoid feeding animals, avoid littering, and respect wildlife safety distances.

Park 02

Uhuru Park

Origin: Uhuru Park is a major public recreational park in Nairobi’s Central Business District, created as a public green space for national gatherings, relaxation, and civic life.

Annual events: National celebrations, public commemorations, civic events, environmental campaigns, family outings, and community gatherings.

Constraints: High visitor numbers during holidays, limited parking, noise restrictions, and protection of lawns, trees, and public facilities.

Rules: Keep the park clean, avoid damaging plants, respect other visitors, avoid unauthorized fires, and obtain approval for organized events.

Park 03

Central Park

Origin: Central Park was developed as an urban green space beside Nairobi’s city centre, offering residents and visitors a peaceful place for rest, exercise, and public events.

Annual events: Public celebrations, cultural activities, fitness events, environmental education, and community meetings.

Constraints: Urban congestion, limited space during major events, maintenance needs, and the need to protect trees and walkways.

Rules: Do not litter, avoid damaging public property, keep noise reasonable, and follow instructions from park authorities.

Park 04

City Park

Origin: City Park is one of Nairobi’s historic green spaces, known for its mature trees, gardens, and role as a public recreational area.

Annual events: Nature walks, tree-planting activities, environmental education, cultural events, and community conservation programmes.

Constraints: Conservation of old trees, maintenance of pathways, protection of biodiversity, and managing visitor impact.

Rules: Protect trees and plants, avoid littering, do not disturb wildlife, and follow designated walking routes.

Park 05

Ngong Road Forest

Origin: Ngong Road Forest is an important urban forest in Nairobi, protected for its ecological value, biodiversity, carbon storage, and recreational importance.

Annual events: Tree-planting days, forest conservation walks, environmental education, and community restoration activities.

Constraints: Encroachment pressure, illegal dumping, fire risk, habitat protection, and controlled access to sensitive forest areas.

Rules: Do not cut trees, dump waste, light fires without permission, or remove plants and animals from the forest.

Park 06

Nairobi Arboretum

Origin: The Nairobi Arboretum was established as a centre for tree research, conservation, education, and public enjoyment. It contains a wide collection of tree and plant species.

Annual events: Nature walks, botanical education, tree-planting campaigns, research activities, and environmental awareness events.

Constraints: Protection of rare plants, controlled pathways, research areas, and limited disturbance to the natural environment.

Rules: Follow the Forests (Nairobi Arboretum) Rules, avoid damaging plants, keep to designated paths, and respect quiet zones.

Gardens Around Nairobi

Nairobi’s gardens add beauty, history, rest, and public life to the city.

Jeevanjee Gardens

Created by Alibhai Mulla Jeevanjee, Jeevanjee Gardens was built in 1904 and donated for public recreation in 1906. It provides a resting space in the Central Business District.

Rules: Protect flowers and trees, avoid littering, keep noise low, and respect other visitors.

Uhuru Gardens

Uhuru Gardens is a national memorial and public garden connected with Kenya’s independence history. It honours national heritage and provides space for reflection and ceremonies.

Rules: Respect monuments and sculptures, avoid damaging plants, and follow event guidelines.

City Park Gardens

The gardens within City Park feature mature trees, shaded walkways, and green spaces for relaxation, learning, and community activities.

Rules: Do not pick flowers, damage trees, litter, or disturb wildlife.
